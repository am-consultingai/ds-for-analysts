?How does the first one work?

Logistic regression gives every feature a weight, adds them up, and turns the total into a chance

score = b₀ + b₁·x₁ + b₂·x₂ + …
chance = 1 / (1 + e−score)

The same model as on Day 1. A plus pushes toward buying, a minus away from it.

Our largest weights
✓ Good

Easy to explain, stable, works with little data

✗ Weak

Each feature counts alone. It can't see “high Analytics and also many products”

?Which model can see such combinations?

A decision tree asks yes/no questions, and the answers put accounts into groups

Two questions, four groups. The share is of the training trials in each group that bought.

✓ Good

Read it as rules. It finds combinations.

✗ Weak

Everyone in a group gets the same chance.

?What goes wrong if we let the tree ask more questions?

A tree with no limit memorises the past, and fails on new months

Too many questions

A tiny group for almost every trial

AUC: 0.50 is a coin flip, 1.00 is perfect

Too few questions

Few groups, so many ties. September 2026:

5 in the best group. Then 29 accounts share one chance, for the last 15 of the 20 leads.

Filled: gets a lead. Empty: left out. The model can't tell them apart, so the account ID decides.

One tree gives a few groups. 300 trees, each 3 questions deep, give every account its own chance: no ties.

✗ Weak

?When should I choose which?

Start simple. Use XGBoost only when you have a lot of data, and it clearly wins

Hundreds of examples

Logistic regression or a small tree

Hard to fool, and easy to explain to the business.

Many thousands of examples, many features

Try random forest and XGBoost

Keep one only if it wins on new months by enough to be worth losing the explanation.

Always compare on new months, against the simple model and against today's rule. On our 360 training trials, XGBoost does not win by enough.

?How does /train score each setting?

Each setting is checked on later months, the same way we split

Fit on the months before, check on the next month. 9 times. Take the average.

Checking on months it learned from measures memory, not skill.

The test months stay closed. They open once, at the end.

?What score does the check give?

AUC: the chance that a random buyer is ranked above a random non-buyer

Take one buyer and one non-buyer at random. Does the model rank the buyer higher? AUC is how often it does.

AUC looks at the whole ranking, so we need no K to compare models.

For the business we still count buyers in the picks and sales a month.

?Is a high AUC enough to trust a model?

No: a model can have a great memory and little skill

Memory = AUC on the trials it learned from. Skill = AUC on months it has never seen. A big gap means it memorised. Never choose a model by its training score.

?So how do we choose the model?

Choose the simplest model whose check score is close to the best

Check scores (AUC, later training months)
1

Close to the best (within 0.02): forest, XGBoost, small tree

2

The simplest of them: the small tree

3

Open the test months once, to confirm: 0.74, no gap

We didn't choose by the test months. If we had, they would no longer be a fair final check.
